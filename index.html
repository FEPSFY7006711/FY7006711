Introduction

   As the current world advances, many automotive engineers are looking to find a way to replace the standard 
petrol and diesel engines currently used. The engineers are looking for a replacement that can reduce the 
amount of emissions and help to tackle climate change. The biggest competitors to these are Battery-Electric
 vehicles, and Hydrogen fuel cells. While both use electric motors, a fuel cell uses hydrogen and oxygen to 
generate electricity, leaving only water as an emission. However an Electric car uses a Lithium Ion battery 
to produce electricity.

Hydrogen Fuel Cell powered cars produce zero CO2 emissions, only producing a bi-product of water (H2O.) 
This trait makes the hydrogen car seem ideal for a replacement to petrol and diesel standard engines in a
 battle against climate change. This project is going to state the reasons on why that may not be accurate.
 Hydrogen Fuel cells in cars have issues which make them impractical. Their traits such as energy losses, 
high costs, limited refuelling infrastructure, storage and competing against battery-electric cars are all 
not yet solved and still need research to be done. Hydrogen cars in mass production would require an entire 
industry shift towards new infrastructure and engineering designs.
